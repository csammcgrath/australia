Flights and the date line Flights & cars booked

Your outbound flight leaves Phoenix Thu Oct 1, 4:00 pm and lands in Sydney Sat Oct 3, 6:50 am via LAX. Crossing the international date line costs you a day, so the plan starts on Oct 3 and keeps that first morning light. The flight home leaves Melbourne Fri Oct 16, 11:00 am. The car goes back the evening before, and you should be at the terminal by about 8 am. Both international legs are confirmed on Delta for two, the domestic flights (Sydney to Cairns, Cairns to Melbourne) are booked, and so are the rental cars in Cairns and Melbourne.

Clocks: Sydney and Melbourne switch to daylight saving overnight from Sat Oct 3 to Sun Oct 4, when clocks go forward an hour. Queensland doesn't use daylight saving, so Port Douglas is an hour behind Sydney and Melbourne for your whole stay. Read every flight and tour time in local time.

Sydney

You won't need a car in Sydney. Ferries, trains and walking cover everything, and city parking is expensive. The hotel is in The Rocks, a few minutes from Circular Quay.

Land, then let the harbour wake you up

You'll be tired from the flight, so keep the day easy. Rooms rarely open before mid-afternoon, so leave your bags at the front desk and walk the harbour foreshore. Daylight and walking help with jet lag.

  • Royal Botanic Garden to Mrs Macquarie's Point, where the Opera House and Harbour Bridge line up in one view
  • Back through Circular Quay to The Rocks for an early dinner, then a Gelato Messina scoop at the Quay on the walk home
Try not to nap for long this afternoon. Staying up until a normal bedtime helps your body clock adjust for the rest of the trip. Clocks go forward an hour tonight, so tomorrow starts an hour earlier than your body expects.

The Manly ferry and Shelly Beach

A relaxed beach day on your first full day, which is also the morning the clocks go forward. Most of it is flat walking, and you can stop and head back whenever you've had enough.

Sunday, hour by hour

Circular Quay to Manly and back, about 5 km of walking (more with North Head)
  • 9:00Walk to Wharf 3 at Circular Quay for the F1 Manly ferry, about 30 minutes across the harbour. Sit outside on the way over for views of the Opera House, the Harbour Bridge and the heads
  • 9:45Off at Manly Wharf and along The Corso, the pedestrian street that runs from the harbour to the ocean beach
  • 10:00Manly Beach to Shelly Beach walk along Marine Parade, about 20 minutes. You pass the small Fairy Bower ocean pool, and the water beside the path is Cabbage Tree Bay, a protected aquatic reserve
  • 10:30Time at Shelly Beach, a sheltered cove that's calmer than the main beach. Swim or snorkel if you've brought gear; the water is around 19°C in early October
  • 11:15Climb up to North Head for the Fairfax Walk and its lookouts across the harbour to the city skyline. It's a longer walk uphill from Shelly Beach, so a short taxi ride up is fine if you're tired optional
  • 12:30Lunch (see options below), then gelato on The Corso
  • 2:30Ferry back to Circular Quay. Spend a slow afternoon in Barangaroo or Surry Hills, or rest at the hotel
  • 6:00Easy dinner: Spice Alley in Chippendale or Chat Thai in Haymarket, then an early night before the walk
Paying for transportTap on and off with the same contactless card or phone for every trip. Sydney caps fares per day, and Sundays and public holidays have a lower cap of around AU$10, so the ferry there and back plus a bus later in the day cost about the same as one return trip.
Lunch in ManlyThe Boathouse Shelly Beach sits at the end of the walk; it's walk-in only and very busy on weekends, so go before noon. Hugos on the wharf does pizza and seafood near the ferry. Cibaria, a hatted beachfront Italian, is the nicer option and worth booking.
GelatoAnita Gelato at 46 The Corso is the top-rated gelato shop in Manly on Tripadvisor, and the Cibaria team recommends its own pistachio gelato with sea salt.
BringSunscreen, hats and water. The UV is strong in October even when the air feels cool. Swimsuits and a towel if you want to swim at Shelly.

Harbour Rocks

Bondi to Coogee coastal walk

The coastal walk is about 6 km one way, past Bondi, Tamarama, Bronte, Clovelly, Gordons Bay and Coogee. NSW's tourism site puts it at about two hours at a good pace; with swims, detours and lunch it fills a relaxed day. It's mostly paved, but there are steep sections and plenty of stairs. Walking from Bondi toward Coogee is the slightly easier direction.

Monday, hour by hour

Bondi to Coogee, about 6 km on foot plus buses
  • 8:00Bus 333 from Circular Quay to Bondi Beach, about 40 minutes. It's a public holiday, so buses may run on a Sunday timetable; check the Opal or TripView app
  • 8:45Breakfast near Hall Street: bills (the original Bondi café, known for its ricotta hotcakes) or one of the cafés on Campbell Parade. Walk along the beachfront to the south end
  • 9:30Start at Bondi Icebergs, the ocean pool at the south end of the beach. A swim there costs a small entry fee optional
  • 9:45Around the cliffs to Tamarama, a small, steep cove, about 15 minutes in
  • 10:15Bronte: take the short detour to the Bronte Baths ocean pool and lookout, which the main path skips. Toilets and a café strip here
  • 10:45Past Waverley Cemetery, built right on the clifftop. Walk the boardwalk along its seaward edge
  • 11:15Clovelly, a long, narrow inlet with calm water. It's the best spot on the walk for a swim or snorkel
  • 11:45Gordons Bay, then over Dunningham Reserve. Leave the paved path for the sandstone near the cliff edge if you want a better view down the coast
  • 12:15Arrive at Coogee. Lunch, a swim at the beach or Wylie's Baths (paid entry), and a rest on the grass
  • 2:30Bus 373 from Coogee back to Circular Quay, about 45 minutes, or a rideshare if you're tired
  • 7:00Dinner: a good night for your Quay or Bistecca booking. Check they're open on the public holiday
Public holiday crowdsOct 5 is Labour Day in NSW, the last day of a long weekend, so the path and the beaches will be busy by late morning. Starting at Bondi by 9:30 keeps you ahead of most of it. Transport fares fall under the lower holiday cap.
Ocean poolsThe route passes several. Icebergs and Wylie's Baths charge entry; Bronte Baths and the Clovelly inlet are free. McIvers Baths, just past Wylie's at Coogee, is for women and children only.
Food along the wayBondi has the most choice at the start (Gelato Messina is on Hall Street if you want a scoop first). Bronte and Clovelly have cafés and kiosks. Coogee has a strip of cafés and fish-and-chip shops along Arden Street.
BringShoes with grip, since the sandstone and stairs can be slippery. Sunscreen, hats, water, swimsuits and a towel. There's little shade between Bronte and Clovelly.

Daintree & Cape Tribulation

A full self-drive day in two parts. Mossman Gorge is the southern end of the Daintree, close to Port Douglas. North of the Daintree River ferry, a sealed road winds about 35 km through rainforest to Cape Tribulation, with short boardwalks and beaches along the way. Times below are approximate.

Thursday, hour by hour

Port Douglas to Cape Tribulation and back, about 230 km
  • 7:30Leave Port Douglas. Mossman Gorge Cultural Centre is about 20 km and 22 minutes north
  • 8:00Mossman Gorge. You can't drive or walk to the gorge itself, so park at the Centre and take the electric shuttle (every 15 min from 8 am, $15.50 per adult, unlimited rides that day). Walk the boardwalk and the rainforest circuit; most of the self-guided walks take between 5 and 45 minutes
  • 10:00Drive north to the Daintree River ferry, about 50 km from Port Douglas. The crossing takes around 5 minutes
  • 10:00Daintree River croc cruise before the ferry, about 1 to 1.25 hours (Solar Whisper and others run from the south bank). Adds time, so drop a boardwalk later if you do it optional
  • 10:30Walu Wugirriga (Mt Alexandra) Lookout at the top of the range, looking back over the river mouth to Snapper Island
  • 10:50Daintree Discovery Centre (paid entry) for the 23 m canopy tower and aerial walkway. If you'd rather skip the fee, the free Jindalba Boardwalk next door has a 700 m short loop
  • 12:15Marrdja Boardwalk at Noah Beach, about 30 minutes through fan palms and mangroves
  • 1:00Lunch at Cape Tribulation, then the 350 m Kulki walk to the lookout and down onto Cape Tribulation Beach. Add the 1.2 km Dubuji Boardwalk to Myall Beach if you have the energy
  • 2:45Turn around. Stop at the Daintree Ice Cream Co. on the way south (closes late afternoon)
  • 3:45Ferry back across the river, home to Port Douglas by about 5, before sunset at roughly 6 pm
FerryRuns continuously from 5 am to midnight every day. Buy tickets online ahead for a QR code, or pay by EFTPOS or cash at the ferry. A locals' priority lane runs 9 am to 4:30 pm in busy periods, so expect some wait mid-morning.
Crocodiles and swimmingSaltwater crocodiles live in the Daintree River, its creeks and along these beaches. Don't swim at Cape Tribulation or Myall Beach, and stay back from creek edges. Obey the signs at Mossman Gorge, where currents get strong after rain.
CassowariesYou may see one crossing the road or near the boardwalks. Slow down, keep your distance and never feed them. They're large birds and can injure people.
Fuel and signalFill up in Port Douglas or Mossman. There's a service station at Cow Bay north of the ferry, but phone coverage is patchy, so download offline maps before you go.
Start early and aim to be back before dark, when wildlife wanders onto the roads. The road is sealed as far as Cape Tribulation, so turn around there. The Bloomfield Track beyond is 4WD-only and most rental agreements don't cover it.

Atherton Tablelands and the waterfalls

This is the long inland driving day, with about 4 to 5 hours behind the wheel in total. The Tablelands sit on a plateau inland from Cairns, cooler and greener than the coast, with crater lakes, dairy farms and a loop of waterfalls near Millaa Millaa. Times below are approximate.

Friday, hour by hour

Port Douglas to Millaa Millaa and back, about 300 km
  • 7:30Leave Port Douglas inland via Mossman, Mount Molloy and Mareeba. Atherton is roughly 1 hr 45 away
  • 9:30Gallo Dairyland, between Atherton and Malanda (open 9 to 5). Watch cheese and chocolate being made through the factory windows, and buy chocolate for later
  • 10:30Curtain Fig Tree near Yungaburra: a strangler fig whose roots hang about 15 m to the ground, with a boardwalk around it. Allow 20 minutes
  • 11:00Lake Eacham, a clear volcanic crater lake. Swim off the main picnic area, or walk the 3 km track around the lake
  • 11:00Yungaburra platypus platform on Peterson Creek. Platypus are most active early and late in the day, so a sighting at midday is luck optional
  • 12:45Lunch at Mungalli Creek Dairy (open 10 to 4), about 10 minutes from the falls
  • 1:45The Waterfall Circuit, a 17 km sealed loop on Theresa Creek Rd. Start at Millaa Millaa Falls, a short walk down from the car park to a deep, cold pool you can swim in. Then Zillie Falls (viewing platform at the top, no swimming) and Ellinjaa Falls (walk down to the base; look for platypus and turtles). Allow 2 to 3 hours with a swim
  • 3:45Head back the way you came, home by about 6 pm around sunset
Swimming gearBring swimsuits, towels and water shoes. The water at Millaa Millaa Falls is cold even in warm weather, and the rocks at the edge are slippery. There are no crocodiles at Lake Eacham or the falls.
Food timingGallo's café kitchen serves meals until about 2 pm on weekdays, and Mungalli closes at 4. Pack water and snacks for the circuit, which has no shops.
RoadsThe route via Mareeba is the gentler drive. The Gillies Range road south of the Tablelands has over 260 bends and is best avoided if either of you gets carsick. The whole Waterfall Circuit is sealed and fine in a regular car.
Make it shorterIf you'd rather not do the full day, skip Gallo and the platypus stop and go straight to Lake Eacham and the falls. If it rains hard, the waterfalls are more dramatic but the swimming holes can get muddy and fast.
Go if swimming under waterfalls appeals. If you'd rather have a slow day, swap this one for a beach day.

Twelve Apostles booking from Oct 16A new visitor centre opens Oct 16, and from that day every visitor must pre-book. Your visits (Oct 14–15) fall just before, so no booking should be needed. The booking system goes live Oct 5, so check twelveapostles.org.au that week in case the transition affects the old lookout. Gibson Steps and Loch Ard Gorge aren't affected.
Roadworks on the way homeThe coastal road's landslip repairs finished in August, but work continues on the inland roads out of Port Campbell (Cobden, Timboon and Colac–Lavers Hill) until late 2026. Crews work weekdays, so expect some single-lane sections and lower speed limits on Thursday. Timboon–Port Campbell Rd was closed from Sep 16 for about three weeks; if it's still shut, follow the detour via Cobden.
Driving the roadKeep left, and use the pull-over bays to let faster locals pass. Never stop on the road itself for a koala. Wildlife is busiest at dawn and dusk, so take the 12 km between the Apostles and Port Campbell slowly after sunset.
Fuel & signalFill up in Apollo Bay. Fuel is sparse through the Otways, and there's only a small stop at Lavers Hill. Phone coverage drops out in the forest, so download offline Google Maps for the whole route before you leave Melbourne.
WeatherOctober on this coast is roughly 12–20°C, windy and changeable. It can be sunny at Lorne and drizzling at the Apostles. Keep a warm layer and a rain jacket in the car. Check VicTraffic and the VicEmergency app the morning of each drive.
Rental carEvery road on this plan is sealed, so a regular car is fine. Note your depot's closing time for Thursday. If you're running late, the airport depots usually have an after-hours key drop, but confirm that it exists.

Before you fly

Apply for an Australian ETA for both passports (via the official AustralianETA app) if you haven't already. You can't board without it.
Book dinners now: Quay (weeks out), Salsa & Melaleuca, plus Bistecca, Zinc, Supernormal, Tipo 00 and Graze.
Re-read every booking in local time, since Queensland is an hour behind Sydney and Melbourne during your trip.
Pack swimsuits, reef-safe sunscreen and grippy shoes for the Sydney beach days, the reef and the waterfalls.
You drive on the left in Australia, so take the first hour slowly.
Check track and market hours the week before, since closures and holidays change them.
